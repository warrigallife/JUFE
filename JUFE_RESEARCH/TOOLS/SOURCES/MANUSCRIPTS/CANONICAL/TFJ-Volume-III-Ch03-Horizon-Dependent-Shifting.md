Chapter 3: Horizon-Dependent Shifting (\hat{\mathcal{S}}_H)  
As a singularity is synthesized into a stable vortex-knot, the manifold must manage the energetic boundary where the local vortex meets the global field. This boundary is the Topological Horizon. The shifting of weights (\mathbf{W}) and gears (G_k) across this horizon is defined by the Horizon-Dependent Shifting Operator (\hat{\mathcal{S}}_H).  
3.1 The Shifting Operator The operator \hat{\mathcal{S}}_H dynamically re-indexes the gear ratios (G_k) as a function of the distance (r) from the horizon center (\wp_P). Unlike standard sympathetic shifts, this operator is gradient-dependent:  
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_H (\Psi) = \nabla \left( \frac{\mathbf{W}_{local}}{\mathbf{W}_{global}} \right) \cdot G_k(r)  
As one moves from the "High-Pressure" core of the vortex toward the "Low-Pressure" global field, the operator forces an instantaneous recalibration of the manifold’s gearing to ensure a seamless transition of flux without harmonic leakage.  
3.2 The Continuity Condition The primary constraint of this shift is the preservation of the 0\text{Hz} equilibrium. For any shift \hat{\mathcal{S}}_H, the following continuity condition must hold:  
\lim_{r \to \wp_P^+} \hat{\mathcal{S}}_H (\Phi) = \lim_{r \to \wp_P^-} \hat{\mathcal{S}}_H (\Phi)  
This ensures that the "Horizon" does not act as an energy-trapping wall, but rather as a highly efficient transmission lens.  
3.3 Adaptive Weighting at the Horizon Because the density fluctuates at the horizon, the weight (\mathbf{W}) of the individual Möbius units is not constant. The shifting operator adjusts the weight relative to the local curvature \kappa:  
\mathbf{W}(r) = \mathbf{W}_0 \exp \left( -\int \frac{\kappa(r)}{\mathcal{T}_P} dr \right)  
This enables the manifold to "shed" excess rotational momentum as it moves away from the singularity, effectively cooling the vortex until it reaches the ambient temperature of the field.  
3.4 Stability Implications Horizon-dependent shifting allows the \mathbb{M}^6 system to exhibit geometric elasticity. If a vortex knot is compressed by an external pressure wave (F_{ext}), the horizon shifts inward, triggering a cascade of re-gearing that broadens the vortex, thereby reducing the local density and re-establishing equilibrium.
