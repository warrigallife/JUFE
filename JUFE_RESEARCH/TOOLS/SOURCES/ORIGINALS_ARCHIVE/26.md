Chapter 1: The Mechanics of Transmission  
  
Volume II delineates the transformation of static harmonic potential into dynamic kinetic flux. In the \mathbb{M}^6 system, this transmission is not a lossy process but a geared resonance shift.  
  
1.1 The Kinetic Coupling Equation The transmission of flux \Phi_\alpha through the manifold is governed by the Kinetic Coupling (\mathcal{K}), which modulates the rotational velocity \vec{v} of the topological vortex knots based on the local gear ratio G_k:  
  
\mathcal{K} = \sum_{\alpha \in \mathbf{\Omega}} \left( \frac{\Phi_\alpha}{G_k} \right) \cdot \nabla \vec{v}_\alpha  
  
This equation defines the "mechanical advantage" of the field. When G_k increases, the manifold decreases its angular rotation speed while increasing its structural torque, allowing it to maintain the 0\text{Hz} state despite external resistance.  
  
1.2 Geared Quadrature Quadrature, in this context, is the process of mapping an arbitrary flux geometry \mathbf{P}_n onto the orthogonal base-grid of the manifold. We define the transformation matrix \mathbf{T} such that:  
  
\mathbf{T} (\mathbf{P}_n) \to \mathbf{S}_q  
  
where \mathbf{S}_q is the square-harmonic eigenstate of the system. This transformation requires the simultaneous adjustment of gear ratios G_k across the affected junctions to preserve the total field energy.  
  
1.3 Sympathetic Gear Modulation To maintain stability during high-load transmission, the manifold employs sympathetic modulation:  
  
\delta G_k = -\beta \sum_{\beta \neq \alpha} \mathbf{E}_{\alpha \beta} \cdot \delta \mathcal{T}_\beta  
  
where \beta is the coupling sensitivity. This ensures that a gear shift at one junction is compensated by an inverse shift at all connected junctions, preventing harmonic "backlash" or energy leakage.  
  
Chapter 2: Energy Conservation and Flux Stability

\int_{\mathbb{M}^6} \nabla \cdot (\mathbf{W} \Phi) \, dV \equiv 0

Deviations from this peak efficiency trigger the Hysteresis Operator (\hat{\mathcal{H}}_y), signaling the manifold to revert to a previously established efficient gear-configuration.
