# Purpose

This document introduces the name **Éire**, the Irish name for Ireland, and explains its historical, linguistic, and mythological significance.

Understanding the name Éire provides an important foundation for later studies of Irish mythology and national identity.

---

# Modern Meaning

**Éire** is the modern Irish-language name for Ireland.

In English, the country is called **Ireland**.

In Irish, it is called **Éire**.

---

# Linguistic Origin

The name Éire derives from **Ériu**, a figure preserved in medieval Irish literature.

The relationship between the mythological figure and the place name reflects the close connection between landscape, identity, and tradition within early Irish culture.

---

# Mythological Tradition

According to the medieval literary tradition, Ériu is one of three sisters associated with the island of Ireland.

The Milesians encounter Ériu before taking possession of the island.

She requests that the island forever bear her name.

The tradition explains the literary origin of the name Éire.

This account belongs to Ireland's medieval mythological tradition rather than established historical fact.

---

# Historical Perspective

The name Éire has been used in Irish for many centuries.

Modern historical linguistics traces the name to much older Celtic forms, although the precise development remains the subject of scholarly research.

---

# Repository Note

Throughout this repository:

- **Éire** refers to the Irish-language name of Ireland.
- **Ériu** refers to the mythological figure preserved in medieval literature.

Although closely related, they should not automatically be treated as identical concepts.
