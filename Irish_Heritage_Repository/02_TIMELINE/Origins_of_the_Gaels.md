# Purpose

This document serves as the starting point for the Irish Heritage Repository.

Rather than beginning with mythology or later historical traditions, this document examines the earliest available evidence concerning the origins of the Gaels and the development of Gaelic culture.

The objective is to distinguish clearly between:

- Archaeological evidence
- Linguistic evidence
- Early Irish tradition
- Medieval literature
- Modern interpretation

This document will evolve as additional evidence and sources are reviewed.

---

# Why Begin Here?

Many discussions of Irish history begin immediately with the Tuatha Dé Danann, the Book of Invasions, or the Tree of Life.

While these traditions are culturally significant, they are best understood after establishing the broader historical and linguistic background from which they emerged.

This repository therefore begins with the origins of the Gaels before progressing into mythology, philosophy, language, and symbolism.

---

# Working Principles

The Irish Heritage Repository follows several guiding principles.

## 1. Primary Sources Lead

Whenever possible, priority will be given to:

- Early Irish manuscripts
- Archaeological evidence
- Historical linguistics

---

## 2. Mythology and History are Recorded Separately

Mythology is not treated as historical fact.

Likewise, mythology is not dismissed simply because it contains supernatural elements.

Each tradition is examined within its own cultural and historical context.

---

## 3. Modern Interpretations are Identified

Many modern books and websites combine historical evidence with later folklore or contemporary spiritual interpretations.

Where this occurs, the distinction will be recorded.

---

## 4. Uncertainty is Acceptable

Where historians disagree, or where evidence is incomplete, uncertainty will be documented rather than hidden.
