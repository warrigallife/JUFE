# Purpose

This document explains the meaning of the term **Celt** and its relationship to the Gaelic tradition.

Understanding this distinction provides important context for Irish history, language, and mythology.

---

# Short Definition

A Celt is a member of one of the peoples associated with the Celtic languages and related cultural traditions.

"Celt" is a broad cultural and linguistic term.

"Gael" refers specifically to the Gaelic (Goidelic) branch of that wider family.

---

# The Celtic Language Family

The surviving Celtic languages are divided into two major branches.

## Goidelic (Gaelic)

- Irish (Gaeilge)
- Scottish Gaelic (Gàidhlig)
- Manx (Gaelg)

## Brittonic

- Welsh (Cymraeg)
- Cornish (Kernewek)
- Breton (Brezhoneg)

---

# Important Distinction

Every Gael is part of the wider Celtic family.

However, not every Celt is a Gael.

For example:

- Welsh traditions are Celtic but not Gaelic.
- Breton traditions are Celtic but not Gaelic.
- Irish traditions are both Celtic and Gaelic.

---

# Historical Note

The ancient peoples of Europe who spoke Celtic languages were diverse and lived across large parts of the continent.

Modern scholarship recognises that "Celtic" describes a family of languages and associated cultural traditions rather than a single unified nation or tribe.

---

# Repository Note

Throughout this repository, the word "Celt" refers to the broader cultural and linguistic family unless otherwise specified.
