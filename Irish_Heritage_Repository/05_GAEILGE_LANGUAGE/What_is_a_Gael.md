# Purpose

This document introduces the meaning of the word **Gael**, one of the most important terms in Irish history and culture.

Understanding this term provides the foundation for studying Irish language, mythology, and early history.

---

# Short Definition

A **Gael** is a member of the Gaelic cultural and linguistic tradition.

Historically, being a Gael referred primarily to language and culture rather than modern nationality.

---

# The Gaelic Languages

The Gaelic (Goidelic) languages include:

- Irish (Gaeilge)
- Scottish Gaelic (Gàidhlig)
- Manx (Gaelg)

These form one branch of the wider Celtic language family.

---

# Gael versus Celt

Not every Celt is a Gael.

The Celtic languages are divided into two major branches.

## Goidelic (Gaelic)

- Irish
- Scottish Gaelic
- Manx

## Brittonic

- Welsh
- Cornish
- Breton

Every Gael belongs to the wider Celtic family.

However, Welsh, Cornish and Breton traditions are not Gaelic traditions.

---

# Early Tradition

Medieval Irish literature associates the Gaels with Goídel Glas, a legendary ancestor.

This tradition belongs to Ireland's medieval origin literature and should be studied within its literary and cultural context rather than treated automatically as established history.

---

# Repository Note

Throughout this repository the term **Gael** refers primarily to the Gaelic cultural and linguistic tradition unless otherwise specified.
