# Purpose

This document introduces the major medieval Irish manuscripts that preserve much of Ireland's early mythology, literature, genealogy, history, poetry, and law.

Although many of the stories they contain are far older than the manuscripts themselves, the surviving written versions were copied by medieval scribes, many of whom were Christian monks.

These manuscripts form the foundation upon which much of our knowledge of early Irish tradition is based.

---

# Why These Manuscripts Matter

When studying Irish mythology, history, or philosophy, it is important to distinguish between:

- the surviving medieval manuscripts,
- the traditions they preserve,
- modern translations,
- and later interpretations.

This repository gives priority to the earliest available written sources whenever possible.

---

# Major Irish Manuscripts

## Lebor na hUidre
**Translation:** *The Book of the Dun Cow*

One of the oldest surviving Irish manuscripts.

Compiled during the twelfth century, it preserves some of the earliest versions of important Irish stories, including material from the Ulster Cycle.

---

## Book of Leinster

Compiled during the twelfth century.

Contains mythology, genealogy, poetry, historical traditions and one of the principal versions of the *Lebor Gabála Érenn* (The Book of the Taking of Ireland).

---

## Book of Ballymote

Compiled around the late fourteenth century.

Contains mythology, genealogies, historical material, Ogham traditions and other important texts.

---

## Yellow Book of Lecan

A fourteenth-century manuscript containing mythology, heroic tales, genealogies and religious material.

It overlaps with several earlier traditions while preserving unique texts.

---

## Lebor Gabála Érenn

Usually translated as:

**The Book of the Taking of Ireland**

Rather than being a single ancient eyewitness history, this is a medieval literary compilation that presents Ireland's mythological history through a sequence of successive invasions.

It is one of the most important sources for understanding Irish origin traditions.

---

# Repository Principles

This repository distinguishes between:

- Medieval manuscripts
- Earlier oral traditions
- Archaeological evidence
- Historical linguistics
- Modern scholarship

Each contributes differently to our understanding of early Ireland.

---

# Important Note

The age of a manuscript is not necessarily the age of the tradition it records.

Many stories were transmitted orally for centuries before being written down.

Determining the antiquity of those traditions remains an active area of scholarly research.
